Overview

Burhanpur town, founded in 1400 AD by the Faruqi ruler, Nasir Khan on the northern banks of the Tapti river, and strategically critical throughout medieval Indian History to any king who wished to rule the Deccan, bears traces of its dramatic past in its crowded bylanes and sprawling, often ruined, suburbs.

The casual visitor may be surprised by how much there is to see here. In fact, at almost every turn in the older part of town, now known as 'Seetee' there seems to be a medieval mosque or sarai, or gurudwara or temple, a towering gateway or a solitary grave. Its immediate outskirts, too, within a 20 - odd km radius offer the massive Asirgarh Fort to the north, the luxurious pleasure palace of Malal Gulara to the east and the sacred Icchadevi Temple to the south, with numerous, old dargahs, temples, and medieval ruins on the way.

Indeed though, Burhanpur may appear at first glance to be little more than average small town, to those who care to peel away its layers, it offers a glimpse into a truly glorius past.

A past in which great battles were lost and won epic love flourished and dies, ambitious journeys both political and spiritual, were embarked upon, and formidable architecture feats were accomplished to engrave the town's name forever in history.

Attractions

  • Jama Masjid- over 36m high mminars tower over the mosque arched compound and bustling bazaar outside.
  • Kali Masjid- (black mosque) it is looted in the Daulatpura Mohalla, a 10 min walk uphill from the Rajghat along the Tapti.
  • Bibi Ki Masjid- it was built between 1520 and 1540 by a Faruqi queen.
  • Tombs of Nadir Shah and Adil Shah- about 3kms from the center of the town are the tombs of several Faruqi rulers and their queens, set within a walled enclosure.
  • Badshahi Qila- Adil Khan II propelled the construction of a citadel, with walls pierced by eight gates stretching around Burhanpur.
  • Zenana Hammam- located within the Badshahi Qila. Built in a combination of Mughal and Persian styles.
  • Kundi Bhandara- Burhanpur boasts of one of the most elaborate system for transporting water demonstrating an unparalleled construction technique.
  • Tomb of Shah Nawaz Khan- On the northern outskirts of Burhanpur, about 2km from the town, is a beautiful Mughal monument, on the banks of Utaoli river.
  • Bilquis Jahan- the tomb is locally known as Kharboozi (melon like) Gumbaz. Shah Jahan's second son erected this tomb in memory of his wife Bilquis.
  • Malal Gulara- located on the banks of Badi Utaoli River is Malal Gulara, 21 km from Burhanpur on Amravati Road, north of the village of Sindhkheda.
  • Hazrat Shah Bhikari- very close to the tomb of Bilquis Jahan is the dargah of Hazrat Shah Bhikari also known as Hazrat Shah Nizamuddin.
  • Asirgarh-
    • Perched high on a hill of the Satpura range just under 25 km north of Burhanpur is one of the most magnificent forts of the region, rivalling even the great Golconda in impregnability. Alexander Cunningham attributes the origins of the fort to 'great tranquility', going so far as to identify with the 'Ozoabis' mentioned by Ptolemy.

People

Shah Jahan & Mumtaz Mahal in Burhanpur
Of the Mughals, Shah Jahan is perhaps most closely connected to the city. His first daughter, Roshanara, was born here in 1617, When Shah Jahan was still a prince Latu, as emperor, Shah Jahan returned to Burhanpur, where he lost the wife he passionately loved, Mumtaz Mahal: She died while delivering their 14th child, also a daughter in Burhanpur.



Sikhism in Burhanpur
The Sikh connection in Burhanpur dates as far back as the early 16th century when Guru Nanak is said to have visited the town.(Surjit Singh Gandhi, History of the Sikh Gurus Retold 1606 - 1708 CE).
The gathering to which he is said to have preached, on the banks of the Tapti,is commemorated by the Gurudwara Sangat Rajghat Patshali Pahili, which still stands at the site.

Travel

  • By Air- The nearest airport is Devi Ahilya Bai International Airport at Indore (180km)
  • By Rail- Burhanpur has its own railway station, 8km from Gandhi Chowk
  • By Road- Regular bus services connect Burhanpur with Indore
